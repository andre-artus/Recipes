# Lamb Döner Kebab for Two

**Serves** 2 (2 wraps)  
**Chilling** 30 minutes  
**Cooking** about 25–30 minutes

Adapted from [Middle Eats’ döner kebab video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W1OB20BOIEQ). This batch uses 300 g of lamb mince, or 150 g per person, with the seasonings scaled to about 30% of the original and rounded for measuring.

## Ingredients

### Döner meat

- 300 g cold lamb mince, about 30% fat
- ½ medium onion, finely grated
- 1 Tbsp + ½ tsp plain yoghurt
- ½ tsp + a pinch fine salt
- ⅛ tsp MSG
- ½ tsp + a pinch ground cumin
- ½ tsp + a pinch dried oregano
- ¼ tsp ground black pepper
- 1¼ tsp pul biber (Turkish red pepper flakes)
- 2 tsp olive oil to start, up to 2 Tbsp + 1 tsp if the mince is lean
- 1 tsp butter, for searing

### To serve

- 2 soft flatbreads, such as [Turkish lavash](../bread/Turkish%20Lavash.md)
- 1 batch [Chilli Ezme](Chilli%20Ezme.md)
- 1 batch [Tomato and Parsley Salad](Tomato%20and%20Parsley%20Salad.md)
- 1 batch [Pickled Red Cabbage](Pickled%20Red%20Cabbage.md)
- 1 batch [Döner Garlic Sauce](Döner%20Garlic%20Sauce.md)

## Method

1. In a food processor, blend the grated onion, yoghurt, salt, MSG, cumin, oregano, black pepper, pul biber and 2 tsp olive oil. Add the cold mince and process until the mixture is sticky and stretches in strands. If using leaner mince, add more oil a teaspoon at a time as needed. Cover and chill for 30 minutes.
2. Heat the oven to **150°C**. Divide the mixture between two sheets of baking paper. Cover each with a second sheet and roll it out with a rolling pin to an even layer about 3–5 mm thick. Peel off the top sheet. Starting at a short edge, roll up the meat and its bottom sheet together so the paper separates the layers. Set both rolls seam-side down on a rimmed baking tray.
3. Bake for about 22 minutes, checking the smaller rolls a few minutes early. The centre of each roll should reach **71.1°C (160°F)**. Carefully unroll, peel away the paper and tear or slice the meat into bite-sized strips. [USDA ground meat temperature guidance](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/What-is-a-safe-internal-temperature-for-cooking-meat-and-poultry)
4. Melt the butter in a wide frying pan over medium-high heat. Sear the strips in a single layer, in batches if needed, turning until the edges brown. If the pan looks dry, add a splash of the tray juices and let it bubble away.
5. Warm the flatbreads. Spread with ezme, add the tomato salad and drained cabbage, then pile on the lamb and spoon over the garlic sauce. Fold and serve straight away.
