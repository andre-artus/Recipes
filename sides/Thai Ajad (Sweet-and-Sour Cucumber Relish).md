# Thai Ajad (Sweet-and-Sour Cucumber Relish)


Ajad (also spelled ajat or achat) is a traditional Thai cucumber relish served with yellow curry, satay and other rich dishes. It combines crisp cucumber, shallots and chilli in a lightly sweetened vinegar dressing.

&#x20;10 min preparation

&#x20;15–30 min resting

3–4 servings

## Ingredients

Cucumber — ½ large English cucumber (150–200 g)

Shallots — 2 small, thinly sliced

Red chilli — 1 small, thinly sliced

White vinegar or rice vinegar — 60 ml (4 Tbsp)

White sugar — 35–40 g (about 3 Tbsp)

Water — 60 ml (4 Tbsp)

Salt — ¼ tsp

## Method

1\. Prepare the dressing. Combine vinegar, sugar, water and salt in a small saucepan. Heat gently, stirring until the sugar dissolves. Do not boil for long.

2\. Cool completely. Remove from the heat and allow the dressing to reach room temperature. This keeps the cucumber crisp.

3\. Prepare the vegetables. Cut the cucumber lengthwise into quarters, then slice into pieces approximately 3–5 mm thick. Thinly slice the shallots and red chilli.

4\. Combine. Place the cucumber, shallots and chilli in a bowl. Pour over the cooled dressing.

5\. Rest. Refrigerate for 15–30 minutes before serving, allowing the flavours to develop.

6\. Serve chilled in a small bowl alongside your Thai yellow chicken curry and jasmine rice.

## Tips

- Vinegar: Rice vinegar gives a gentler flavour, while ordinary white vinegar produces a sharper, more traditional sweet-and-sour contrast. If using stronger white vinegar, adjust the sugar to taste.
- Cucumber: English cucumber works well. Remove some of the seeds if particularly watery.
- Shallots: If unavailable, use about ¼ of a small red onion, sliced very thinly.
- Chilli: Remove the seeds for a milder relish, or omit the chilli entirely.
- Storage: Best eaten fresh, but can be refrigerated in a covered container for 1–2 days. The cucumber gradually loses its crunch.

## Balancing the flavour

The dressing should be noticeably sweet and sour, with a little saltiness. It is not intended to taste like a strongly pickled cucumber.

For your Blue Elephant yellow curry, I recommend making the ajad slightly more acidic than sweet, especially if using coconut cream rather than coconut milk. The acidity will balance the richness of the curry particularly well.
